## Phase semantics and switching workflow

| Phase | Visible evidence | Limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `move_selection` | Four move rows, including purple rows and green selected-row highlights | Does not establish move execution |
| `pokemon_selection` | Brought-party list and broad Pokémon detail panel | Opening the list does not establish switching intent |
| `switch_selection` | Pokémon-selection layout with a replacement/exit popover | Does not establish that the replacement entered battle |
| `move_target_selection` | Opponent/own target-grid geometry and selected-move banner | Does not establish the move's execution or effect |
| `unknown` | Insufficient visual evidence or unsupported interface | Explicit ambiguity is preserved |

Other existing phases remain supported. `battle_animation` is a tentative battle view without a decision panel and can include waiting. Existing `battle_screen` classification recognizes the battle HUD; the distinction between animation, idle display, and action resolution is not comprehensively validated.

The 268-second move-selection miss came from requiring every row to be purple. Accepting the known green highlight at the same four move-row positions fixes it without a timestamp exception. Pokémon detail viewing is now distinct from the vertically positioned replacement popover. Layout/color evidence is heuristic; other interface profiles may require readable labels or another layout profile.

## Before/after evaluation and coverage

Both recordings were sampled in full at a two-second interval: 362 frames over approximately 722.03 seconds and 268 frames over approximately 534.073 seconds. Final classification and segmentation use those same 630 cached observations. This is full-recording sampled coverage, **not all-frame labeling or full-video accuracy**. Only 31 manually reviewed reference points and three switching transition windows have correctness labels; these are development/review examples, not an independent held-out benchmark.

The original and final predictions are scored against the **same revised annotations**, including the user's 268–282 review and refined phase vocabulary. Original errors include legacy `unknown` target labels and the old broad selection label. The earlier 27/29 score used a different vocabulary and annotation set and is preserved as historical evidence; it is not directly comparable to this table.

Final results:

- No false classification or missed known phase at the 31 reviewed points; all point offsets fall within the scoring tolerance of half the interval plus 0.05 seconds.
- All **3/3 reviewed switching transition windows** match. The broader 270–280 window does not establish an exact transition time. Target-entry/exit transitions exist in the output but lack independently reviewed timing windows, so target-transition accuracy remains unmeasured.
- All **201 final transitions** pass structural checks against ordered neighboring evidence, timestamps, sampling provenance, and uncertainty widths. This verifies internal consistency, not the accuracy of every unlabelled phase or transition.
- All **62/62 sampled observations** inside the two retained reviewed team-preview intervals match. The former shared 270–280 selection interval was superseded by the user's point and transition annotations because it contains two distinct UI phases; its original version is archived.
- The current classifier matches **12/12 benchmark image fixtures**, including both left-anchored target menus. Additional regression tests cover the switching workflow, scaling, ambiguity, short phases, context separation, and OCR caching.
- Temporal smoothing changed **0 samples** in either final timeline; it introduced or suppressed no boundaries in these recordings. Regression tests separately verify that a near-identical unsupported frame can be reconciled while genuine short target interfaces and explicit uncertainty are retained.

The left-banner correction changes 12 cached predictions in the first recording and two in the second from `unknown` to `move_target_selection`. Only the sparse annotated subset has manual correctness labels; these additional unlabelled predictions are not counted as verified accuracy. Final outputs contain 114 and 89 segments respectively, with 113 and 88 transitions.

The remaining 81 `unknown` observations include unsupported or insufficiently evidenced frames. They are not all labeled and cannot all be counted as either correct abstentions or errors. Confidence values are heuristics, not calibrated probabilities. Sampling can miss brief interfaces; layout changes, animated occlusion, unreadable labels, and overlapping HUD/message evidence can still produce classification errors. No complete phase recall, event accuracy, or turn accuracy claim is made.
