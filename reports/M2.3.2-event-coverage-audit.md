# M2.3.2-D: event coverage root-cause audit

Date: 2026-10-14. Diagnostic only. No recognition, parser, deduplication, tracking, or transition algorithms were changed. No videos were decoded, OCR models invoked, existing reports regenerated, or manually inferred events inserted. Only this report is a repository deliverable.

## Findings

The low counts have several distinct causes. The strongest observed upstream loss is the pre-OCR glyph gate: 17 inspected, legible message observations in battles 0/1/2/4 were sampled but not OCRed. The strongest state-correctness loss is missing evidence fusion: later cached HUD identities and HP never enter replay. All 16 video move-execution candidates in those four battles are deferred, including readable moves, because target uncertainty is disallowed. Strict and sometimes late initialization defers additional evidence. Battle 3 is not a comparable recognition benchmark: its 271 candidates include 189 unknowns and dense historical inputs.

Counts below are observations/candidates, not recall, accuracy, distinct battle actions, or complete coverage. Structural success does not establish semantic acceptance.

## 1. What the counters mean

`video_events = len(streams["video"])` in `scripts/m232_replay.py`. Reconstruction merges primary and additional raw observations by `(source_id, sha256)`, fills missing message/log/snapshot payloads, sorts by timestamp, drops empty message text, and collapses adjacent equal whitespace-normalized messages no more than four seconds apart. It then emits one parser candidate per surviving message. No actor/target confidence filter removes candidates at this stage.

- Includes recognized move/switch/faint/stat/field/etc. message types and unresolved `unknown` messages, even when replay later defers them. A multi-Pokémon announcement produces one candidate, not one per Pokémon; a single action may produce several messages.
- Excludes HUD snapshots, HUD HP readings, phase transitions, menu selections, and log-row events. Those payloads are not converted into video events. Initial HUD facts are outside the event count.
- Repeated wording can remain when separated by missing message records or more than four seconds. Different OCR variants of the same announcement remain distinct. This is not an action-count metric.
- Historical raw observations are included for battle 3. No reviewed/manual event fixtures, corrected text overlays, reconciled M2.2.3 events, or M2.3.1 states are imported by M2.3.2 reconstruction. Historical sampling windows were manually chosen; this is a provenance advantage even though the text itself is raw OCR.

## 6. Sampling, selection, parser, and presentation findings

- Survey cadence is about two seconds for all recordings, plus a terminal frame; B0/B1 reuse M1.4 full-video timestamped frames, B3 reuses M2.2.3 survey frames, B2/B4 use 60-second extraction chunks. Two-second sampling can miss short notices, but between-sample loss is **unmeasured** here. No original-video inspection was necessary to prove the sampled misses.
- The message detector counts bright, low-saturation contours in ROI `(0.22, 0.685, 0.64, 0.797)`, requiring contour height >8 and <55% of crop height, area >10, and at least eight qualifying contours. It is neither a character count nor a human-visible-text detector. The reviewed 1311×603 images place legible messages within that ROI; three full-frame overlays confirm this for B0 484.032, B1 314.003, and B4 586.015. Lower contour counts despite readable messages establish a gate failure; the relative contribution of fades, antialiasing, brightness, and contour fragmentation has not been separately benchmarked.
- Phase filters require `battle_message`, `battle_animation`, or `unknown`, time >30s, and prioritize log extraction over message OCR. Diagnostic re-evaluation finds one >=8-glyph phase-blocked non-log frame in B0 (`switch_selection`) and one in B4 (`battle_screen`), none in B1/B2. These two frames were not human-labeled as missed messages; menu/HUD text can also trigger glyph counts. Do not call them true losses without review. Every one of the 17 verified misses is in an allowed phase: the glyph gate, not phase rejection, is decisive.
- Plain `classify` recognizes HUD panels before the fallback phase detector. A selected snapshot uses the older survey phase, while message scheduling uses `visual_phase`. Sparse HUD scheduling (~18s minimum, only on eligible screens) does not cover every transition. Menus containing ability/item/configuration facts are also not automatically battle effect activations.
- Parser emission is permissive: unknown text and ambiguous actors/targets remain candidates. Parsing coverage and replay admissibility are separate funnels. Clear Chinese effect grammar is missing for Knock Off and White Herb; grouped send-outs use only one subject, nickname aliases and trainer names are not populated from cached recognition, and the parser context starts blank instead of being synchronized with HUD evidence.
- Temporal reconstruction dedup removes zero nonempty messages in B0/B1/B2/B4 and 47 in B3. It compares adjacent normalized exact text within four seconds and resets on a row without nonempty message text. No evidence here supports global cross-turn dedup as the sparse-count cause. Because video entries lack turn IDs, a same-text near-boundary false collapse remains an unmeasured risk. Conversely, three raw variants of Sally’s announcement near 652–653s survive in B3, demonstrating that noise can inflate candidates.
- The upstream foreground-signature dedup carries `previous_message` across non-message intervals and resume skips; this is a potential recurrence/resumption weakness, but **zero stored suppression markers** means it is not demonstrated as a cause in these caches.
- Existing HTML event-ID sets exactly equal history event-ID sets for all five video streams. There is no measured candidate disappearance in the exporter. Raw later HUD observations are absent from replay/HTML because they are not converted or attached by reconstruction. HTML retention is not equivalent to applying a candidate; view filtering may hide deferred/unknown events visually.

## 7. Endpoint mismatch diagnoses

### B0: Corviknight vs retained Excadrill

At 138.006667s, `initial_checkpoint` binds only own right Excadrill: own left Tyranitar and both opponents have method `exact`, whereas Excadrill has `text_icon_agreement`. It returns immediately after finding any qualifying Pokémon. At 364.011667s Corviknight is already recorded in `own_active_2` (confidence .999, method `exact`), and remains there at 390.015, 434.023, 500.018, 528.005, 576.013, and 594.017s. Final cached text is 钢铠鸦, HP 152/173. The endpoint frame was visually confirmed to show Corviknight.

Necessary endpoint evidence exists. There are **two separate barriers**: later HUDs are never ingested, and the strict initializer would exclude this `exact` reading anyway. Replay keeps the original Excadrill instance because no subsequent accepted slot transition changes it. Missing Indeedee faint text at 412.003s is another coverage failure, not evidence that Corviknight arrived then; Corviknight was visible earlier. A later HUD can establish observed slot occupancy without asserting an exact send-out time or reusing an instance solely by species.

### B4: Armarouge vs retained Sneasler

Initialization at 160.043333s binds own Indeedee and Sneasler via text/icon agreement; opponents use `exact` and are excluded. The last pre-change active HUD at 384.015s shows right Sneasler. At 432.073333s the cached snapshot shows right Armarouge (confidence .999, `text_icon_agreement`) with 117/162 HP, then 66/162 from 450.125s onward, including 566.011667s. Thus the observation interval is (384.015, 432.073333], not an established exact switch time. The final cached frame was visually confirmed to show Armarouge.

This is not a missing name OCR problem: the required resolution method already passes and both identity and HP survive in JSON. `run` calls `initial_checkpoint` once and supplies only that initial state plus message candidates to `replay_stream`; it never produces later HUD evidence events. Video replay consequently retains Sneasler. The correct fix must preserve instance identity and distinguish observed occupancy from inferred switch/faint causes; copying endpoint state into the tracker would conceal the loss.

Endpoint images: [B0 594.017s](../.cache/m14-phases/sample_battle/00019402.png), [B4 566.012s](../.cache/battles/sample_battle_4/m232-survey/segment-000540/000014.jpg). The two mismatches are confirmed reconstruction gaps; their exact missing transition times/causes remain unresolved.
