Implemented for review on 2026-10-23. Practical is the default CLI/replay profile; Comprehensive is diagnostic. ## Audit, policy and provenance

The [versioned item registry](../damage/data/item-classification-v1.json) covers **all 166 legal canonical items**: 68 standard, 6 evidence-triggered, 9 rare/specialized, and 83 species-specific (81 stones plus Light Ball and Leek). Every entry records canonical ID/name, effect applicability, primary effect, damage/ordering/field relevance, protocol callbacks/messages, lifecycle/history dependencies, forward coverage, recommended policy, uncertainty and exact pinned-source line references. This supplements existing KnowledgeBase identities; it is not a second identity database.

The standard whitelist is an explicit competitive-modeling assumption, **not measured usage frequency**. Utility and defensive items can remain standard even when neutral for one connected hit. Accuracy, recovery, recoil, flinch, switching, residual damage, duration and critical-stage effects are classified from their callbacks, never from absence of a handler. Current HP, status, stages, weather, terrain and critical alternatives must remain explicit. Unimplemented relevant mechanics stay unresolved.

- Practical keeps applicable standard items and compatible stones, groups equivalent items, and leaves trigger/rare/ineffective species-specific alternatives dormant unless explicitly enabled or positively reviewed. A complete negative message window is recorded separately from unreviewed coverage; neither establishes item absence without a verified Champions UI guarantee.
- Comprehensive keeps all legal **applicable** alternatives, including rare items and unresolved histories. Incompatible stones are omitted in both profiles as a species/form candidate-generation restriction; this does not assert that holding an inert wrong stone is legally impossible.
- Light Ball doubles Attack/Sp. Atk only for Pikachu; ineffective holding by another species remains a legal diagnostic alternative. Stones preserve canonical required-item and original-to-Mega ability/training continuity. Meowsticite is mapped to Meowstic from its pinned item-user table; its absent canonical Mega forms are not invented.
- Iron Ball halves Speed and grounds its holder. It remains dormant while ordinary legal Speed parents explain the observation. A late rescue intersects the **original** parent IDs with new Speed survivors and rechecks every prior damage hit; it never grafts an item onto another survivor. Trick Room uses the existing order engine.
- Metronome remains dormant in Practical and explicitly unresolved in Comprehensive: its successive modifiers are 4096, 4915, 5734, 6553, 7372, 8192 over 4096, capped; successful move history/reset state is not implemented.
- Equivalent item identities remain in `item_options`; the solver shares only audited equivalent forward calculations. Different damage supports split rows; later evidence intersects existing six-stat domains. Random rolls and critical alternatives remain independent across hits.

The audit uses the existing pinned Showdown commit `3a766f2cfb5e51c1e428cfe87d0dba91a8f01640`, its base/Champions item tables (SHA-256 and line ranges in the registry), the existing canonical Champions catalog, and the already pinned Champions damage reference. Availability assertions remain community catalog/reference evidence, not new official certification. MIT attribution remains attached to the Showdown sources.

## Reviewed item ledger and causal behavior

`--item-review PATH` accepts an optional JSON object with an `events` list. Each event supplies `battle_id`, stable `individual`, canonical-resolvable `item` name, `timestamp`, `available_at`, `reviewer`, `kind`, and source-frame records (`source_id`, `image`, `sha256`). Kinds are `held`, `activated`, `consumed`, `transferred_in`, `transferred_out`, or `reviewed_negative`; negatives require `coverage_complete`. Quick Claw activation additionally requires the exact `action_id`. Source frames, battle/actor identity and action identity are validated; no new detector or fabricated recognition result is involved.

Possession and activation are separate. The reviewed Quick Claw adapter supplies an activation to the existing deterministic order engine (tested in ordinary ordering and Trick Room); unknown actions remain unknown, and reviewed nonactivation does not exclude possession. The existing sealed Speed support is still the source of action-order constraints: a newly annotated activation that changes an earlier Speed inference needs its corresponding reviewed Speed context, not an invented action queue. No benchmark contains a newly admitted Quick Claw trigger.

Consumption/loss preserves original hidden item identity while changing the action-time held item. At the same timestamp, consumption/transfer-out defaults to **after the action**; `phase: before_action` explicitly changes this. Seed boosts, Unburden and other resulting conditions must be supplied as reviewed action-time state; the direct-hit calculator does not apply them twice. A seed activation/consumption should be annotated as an explicit `consumed` event, not inferred from terrain alone.

A late reveal enables the original item branch and replays the full damage prefix. Item-only availability times also create causal support snapshots, including after the last hit. Earlier snapshots are not backfilled with confirmation. Direct item evidence exclusions, move-membership exclusions, numerical exclusions and policy dormancy have separate fields. Typed-ledger and order-adapter tests are synthetic; actual Champions UI message guarantees remain unresolved.
