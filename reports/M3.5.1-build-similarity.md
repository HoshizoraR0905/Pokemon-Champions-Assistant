## Dissimilarities and mathematical properties

Let B=H+D+R and φγ(t)=exp(γt)−1. Baseline:

`d_SP = λB φγ(|Bx−By|/66) + λD Σ[j∈{H,D,R}] φγ(|xj−yj|/48) + λA φγ(|Ax−Ay|/32) + λC φγ(|Cx−Cy|/32) + λV φγ(|Vx−Vy|/32)`.

Defaults: γ=2, λB=1, λD=.3, λA=1, λC=1, λV=1.2. Scales 66/48/32 are experimental sensitivity choices; 48 is not a legal stat cap. Existing Champions validation requires integer SP in [0,32], six explicitly supplied values and total ≤66; unspent points remain legal.

Alternative: replace the defensive terms with `λB φγ(|Bx−By|/66) + λQ min(Bx,By)/66 ||qx−qy||₁`, q=(H,D,R)/B and λQ=.3. At zero bulk, q is represented as zero and the allocation term is exactly zero whenever either bulk is zero. Offensive terms are identical. We compare both formulations rather than treating either as verified optimal.

All configurations are symmetric, nonnegative and zero on identical builds. These are dissimilarities, not mathematical metrics. With legal Attack values 0,16,32 and all other SP zero: d(0,32)=exp(2)−1≈6.389 > 2(exp(1)−1)≈3.437, violating the triangle inequality under both SP formulations. The min-bulk allocation term alone also fails: (16,0,0) and (0,16,0) have positive allocation distance but both have zero allocation distance through zero bulk. Cosine distance can fail triangle inequalities. Jaccard is a metric on move sets and identity mismatch is a categorical metric; the combined build dissimilarity need not be a metric and can be zero for distinct builds because ability is retained for diagnostics but is not a distance term. No metric pruning or Euclidean clustering is used.

## Learned complete-moveset association

Binary build-by-move incidence preserves all occurrences: N=3388, vocabulary=279 moves. For each move i, fᵢ is its observation count; cᵢⱼ counts builds containing both. Smooth all four cells of each pair’s Bernoulli table with a=.5: pᵢ=(fᵢ+2a)/(N+4a), pᵢⱼ=(cᵢⱼ+a)/(N+4a). Association `min(3,max(0,log(pᵢⱼ/(pᵢpⱼ)))) × cᵢⱼ/(cᵢⱼ+5)`; diagonal is zero. Positive clipping, cap 3 and support shrinkage 5 are explicit regularizers. A zero-support pair receives zero association. 1050 observed pairs have at most two occurrences, out of 1783 observed pairs.

SVD of the symmetric association matrix supplies move embeddings `U[:, :d] sqrt(S[:d])`, default d=16, compared with d=8 and 32. Complete four-move embeddings use mean pooling with a fixed denominator four; the API also supports unit-normalizing each move vector before pooling, but this alternative is not evaluated here. Learned moveset dissimilarity is 1−cosine. Unknown moves contribute zero; a zero-norm comparison has distance one except identical exact move sets have distance zero. Pooling loses interactions: distinct combinations can share an average. Vocabulary, counts, association, SVD and species-specific scales are fitted inside training folds for evaluation. Descriptive full-population fits are reported separately. No language embeddings or manual tactical labels enter training.

Co-occurrence captures empirical association and complementarity, not equivalent function. Common moves can add little discrimination, rare pairs remain uncertain, and equal-weight ESS is not independent evidence. Full frequencies, observed-pair support, shrink factors, singular values and embeddings: [moves.json](../.cache/research/m351/7933afbb341cdbeddbe3/moves.json). Non-observed pairs have c=0 and association=0.

## Combination, normalization and experimental grid

`d_build = βSP dSP/sSP + βmove dmove/sMove + βitem 1[item differs]/sItem + βnature 1[nature differs]/sNature`. Each s is the median strictly positive off-diagonal distance in the training species context; constant components use scale one and contribute zero. Repeated observations influence the pair distribution. Default β=(1,1,0,0); categorical item/nature ablation β=(1,1,.5,.5). Ability is preserved and summarized but is not an extra distance component. Nature is categorical; actual calculated stats are not included.

Thirteen predefined configurations: SP only; SP+Jaccard; SP+learned; learned+item/nature; proportional defensive allocation; γ=1 or 3; defensive suballocation weight .1 or .6; move weight .5 or 2; embedding dimension 8 or 32. All other settings retain defaults. This is a one-factor sensitivity study, not an optimized hyperparameter search.

## Clustering, stability and cluster-count screen

Deterministic PAM BUILD+best SWAP accepts the full precomputed matrix with one unit of mass per occurrence; every medoid is an observed legal build. Average-linkage hierarchy uses occurrence-weighted intercluster means and no Euclidean assumptions. Explore K=1–3 for Hatterene and K=1–4 for the larger contexts; also inspect hierarchy cuts at merge-height quartiles .25/.5/.75. Exact-distance ties use source-order determinism; forced ownership of duplicate medoids only keeps candidate K clusters nonempty.

Three deterministic 80% source-group subsamples refit the global move representation, species scales and clustering. Adjusted Rand indices compare only retained occurrence anchors, not unmatched bootstrap rows. This is limited descriptive stability, not a confidence interval. A conservative display screen keeps K=1 unless a candidate has silhouette≥.25, every cluster at least max(3,ceil(.1N)), and mean ARI≥.65 across all three resamples; take the smallest qualifying K. Thresholds are exploratory assumptions, not validated archetype criteria, and held-out prediction does not tune this screen. All candidates remain inspectable. For informative plots when the screen retains K=1, diagnostic figures show candidate K=3 with the screen result explicitly stated; this is not acceptance of a three-cluster partition.

Move association can alter neighborhoods beyond raw SP, but improvement must be judged target by target against the empirical baseline above. Greater separation or an attractive projection alone is not evidence of better similarity. The study does not establish universal tactical archetypes or an optimal distance. It uses one pinned regulation/source population, a small parameter grid, only three resamples and three grouped folds; pooling conceals interactions and rare-pair uncertainty remains. Defensive decomposition, categorical weights and the cluster-count screen remain modeling assumptions. No Champions mechanics or learnsets were inferred from co-occurrence. No new 52Poké data was needed or retrieved; existing pinned canonicalization, rule validation and form compatibility are reused unchanged.

Concrete assessment: the learned representation identifies observed moveset-associated neighborhoods, including the seven high-Attack Incineroar occurrences, but does not improve any of the four numeric prediction targets over SP-only in these three species. For Incineroar, learned item-identity error is 0.305 versus SP-only 0.381 and training-mode 0.310, a small exploratory gain over the mode. SP+Jaccard has lower numeric error than SP+learned for Incineroar Speed and bulk; neither outperforms SP-only there. Hatterene has no demonstrated predictive gain. Raichu Y has a repeatable default partition but no numeric gain, and its constant Attack/item targets are uninformative. There is insufficient evidence here to replace raw SP neighborhoods with the learned combined distance or accept a universal tactical partition. This negative/mixed result is a completed offline experiment, not a reason to tune weights until the hypotheses hold.

Stop at this offline study. No hierarchical prior, posterior over unknown builds, kernel smoothing, synthetic sampling, strategy recommendation or battle-facing UI integration is implemented.
